# Prose diagnostic taxonomy

## Formulaic scaffolding

- Generic openings: “in recent years,” “with the rapid development of,” “in today's
  evolving landscape,” or an equivalent sentence that contributes no bounded fact.
- Empty importance: “it is important to note,” “it is worth mentioning,” “of particular
  significance,” when the following proposition can stand directly.
- Vague contribution: “offers valuable insights,” “sheds light on,” “bridges the gap,”
  “provides implications” without naming what inference changes.
- Mechanical architecture: every paragraph begins with moreover/however/additionally,
  uses the same length, and ends with a broad significance sentence.
- Repetitive contrast: serial “not only…but also,” “while X, Y,” or three-part lists used
  as rhythm rather than logic.

## Weak intellectual agency

- Dummy subject: “it is believed/considered/argued” without identifying who or what
  evidence warrants the proposition.
- Nominalized action: “the implementation of the examination of” where a concrete actor
  and verb are available.
- Citation shield: a long citation cluster substitutes for explaining the relationship
  among studies.
- Literature ventriloquism: the paragraph reports what sources say but never states the
  synthesis or unresolved disagreement.

## Uncalibrated caution

- Stacked hedges: “may potentially suggest,” “might perhaps indicate,” “appears to seem.”
- Universal caveat: “results should be interpreted with caution” without the exact
  reason or affected inference.
- Claim burial: qualifications occupy most of the sentence before the finding appears.
- Defensive nonclaim accumulation: repeated “we do not claim…” clauses instead of one
  explicit scope statement.

## Rhythm and cohesion

Diagnose repeated sentence openings, uniform lengths, identical paragraph shapes,
excessive parentheticals, abrupt short-sentence chains, and long sentences carrying more
than one argumentative job. Revise according to reasoning structure; do not manufacture
random variation.

## Decision rule

For each candidate ask:

1. What intellectual or evidentiary function does this phrase perform?
2. Would deleting it change the proposition, uncertainty, or logical relation?
3. Can the boundary be named more exactly?
4. Does the revision preserve the author's terminology and disciplinary voice?

Delete or rewrite only when the function is empty, duplicated, obscured, or more
precisely expressible.
