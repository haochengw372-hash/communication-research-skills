# Section logic for communication and social-science manuscripts

## Title and abstract

- Title: name the central phenomenon or mechanism, communication object, and useful
  context; do not promise causality, novelty, or generality beyond the design.
- Abstract: problem/puzzle → verified gap → design/material and sample → primary result
  with appropriate uncertainty → theoretical contribution and boundary.
- A proposal abstract states expected tests, not fabricated findings.

## Introduction

1. Establish a communication problem or empirical puzzle, not a generic technology or
   social-change opening.
2. Show the tension in existing evidence or theory.
3. State the exact missing inference and why it matters.
4. Introduce the mechanism, unit, context, and comparison.
5. State RQs/Hs and preview the evidence-backed contribution.

The introduction must not claim a gap that the literature search did not verify, or a
contribution that the results and discussion cannot discharge.

## Literature and theory

- Synthesize conversations, disagreements, boundary conditions, and measurement choices;
  do not write one summary paragraph per source.
- Give every theory a mechanism and falsifiable implication. Keyword overlap is not a
  theoretical rationale.
- Keep theory, construct, operationalization, and measure distinct.
- End each subsection with what the synthesis licenses for the current RQ/H.
- Present rival explanations before the method so the design can address them.

## Research questions and hypotheses

- Use RQs when direction is genuinely open or the contribution is descriptive,
  interpretive, or exploratory.
- Use directional hypotheses only when theory and prior evidence justify the direction.
- Each RQ/H names the unit, constructs, comparison or relation, and scope condition to
  the degree needed for an answerable test.
- Keep confirmatory and exploratory questions visibly separate.

## Methods

Write for reproducibility and inference: design, population and sampling, units,
materials/data provenance, measures or coding, procedure/collection, analysis,
missingness/exclusions, robustness, ethics, and data/code access. Explain why choices
fit the RQ and construct rather than listing software commands.

## Results

- Follow the preregistered or declared question/model order.
- Report sample flow, measurement or coding quality, primary estimates, uncertainty,
  diagnostics, robustness, and adverse/null results.
- Give denominators, units, analysis population, effect size, interval, and model
  locator where relevant.
- Do not explain why a result occurred unless the genre combines findings and discussion.
- A table or figure does not eliminate the need to state the result, but prose should not
  inventory every cell.

## Discussion

1. Answer each RQ/H without restating the full results.
2. Explain what changes in the theoretical mechanism or boundary condition.
3. Compare rival explanations and unresolved ambiguity.
4. Return to communication institutions, practices, publics, media, or interactions.
5. State methodological and practical implications proportional to the evidence.
6. Give concrete limitations tied to the claim and a feasible next test.

## Conclusion

State the answered question, the field-level inference that changed, and the boundary
that remains. Do not introduce citations, results, constructs, or recommendations that
the manuscript has not developed.
