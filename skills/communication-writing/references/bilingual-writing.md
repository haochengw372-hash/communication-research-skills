# Chinese–English communication-research writing

## Translation contract

Lock construct names, theory names, variable labels, RQ/H numbering, estimands, model
names, statistical notation, citations, quotations, and table/figure identifiers before
translation. Maintain a bilingual terminology table and update it only through an
explicit decision.

## Meaning preservation

- Translate the scientific proposition, not the surface word order.
- Preserve modality and evidence strength: `is associated with`, `predicts`, `affects`,
  and `causes` are not interchangeable.
- Preserve populations, comparisons, time, direction, uncertainty, and negation.
- Do not turn Chinese topic-prominent prose into English paragraphs without an explicit
  controlling claim, or force English nominalizations into unnatural Chinese.
- Keep established theory and construct names aligned with their published translations;
  when several translations exist, record the chosen canonical form.

## Chinese academic prose

Prefer explicit logical relations, stable terminology, and evidence-located claims.
Avoid policy-slogan openings, stacked four-character phrases, inflated significance,
empty “随着……不断发展”, and translating every English passive construction literally.

## English social-science prose

Use topic sentences and known-to-new information flow. Calibrate tense by function and
hedging by evidence strength. Avoid generic “in today's rapidly evolving landscape”,
decorative synonyms, repeated three-part lists, and claims of being the first without a
verified search.

## Bilingual audit

After translation, compare both versions for numbers, signs, statistical thresholds,
RQ/H labels, construct boundaries, causal verbs, limitations, citations, figure/table
references, and declarations. Language polish never authorizes a scientific difference.
