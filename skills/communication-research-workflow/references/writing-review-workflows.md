# Writing, review, journal fit, and finalization

## Contents

1. Drafting and constrained revision
2. Manuscript review and rebuttal
3. Journal fit
4. Citation and submission finalization

## 1. Drafting and constrained revision

**Route:** `communication-writing`; use the available document Skill when the requested
artifact is Word, LaTeX, or another rendered format.

Start with a claim map: research question → theory/mechanism → method/identification → result → limitation → conclusion. Each major claim needs a source: verified literature, authoritative analysis output, or explicitly labeled interpretation.

For constrained revision:

1. lock the permitted sections, word/character budget, terminology, numbers, tables, citations, and “must not change” items;
2. create a before-edit inventory of numerical claims and key terms;
3. edit locally rather than rewriting unrelated sections;
4. compare before/after scope, numbers, samples, estimands, terminology, references, and cross-references;
5. render and visually verify document artifacts when the selected document Skill requires it.

Never fill a missing result with plausible prose. Mark the gap and route back to evidence or analysis.

Use `communication-prose-revision` after the scientific revision is accepted when the
remaining task is formulaic language, defensive over-hedging, paragraph rhythm, or
intellectual agency. It may not silently perform scientific revision.

## 2. Manuscript review and rebuttal

**Route:** `communication-reviewer` for diagnosis; route accepted revisions to
`communication-writing` under a separate edit-authorized contract.

Review in this order:

1. contribution and fit;
2. research question and theory;
3. data provenance, sample, variables, and reproducibility;
4. design, estimand, assumptions, diagnostics, and causal language;
5. results, figures, tables, and numerical consistency;
6. interpretation, limitations, citations, structure, and presentation.

Classify findings as `Critical`, `Major`, or `Minor`. For each, provide location, evidence, consequence, and an actionable remedy. Do not implement a review unless operation and authority explicitly change to execution.

For rebuttals, maintain a point-by-point matrix: reviewer request, interpretation, decision, change, evidence, manuscript location, and unresolved risk. Do not claim a change was made until verified in the manuscript.

## 3. Journal fit

**Route:** `communication-reviewer` plus current official venue sources; use
`communication-writing` only after the venue decision is accepted.

Require at least title/abstract, contribution, method, article type, audience, constraints, and preferences. Verify current information from primary official sources:

- indexing status and exact edition/date;
- ranking system and category—do not mix JCR quartile, SJR quartile, CAS partition, or local lists;
- aims and scope, recent relevant articles, article type;
- word limits, formatting, review model, data/code policies, fees, and submission requirements.

Record access date and uncertainty. If current subscription-only ranking data is unavailable, request an official export or mark it unverified; do not substitute stale data. Recommend a portfolio with fit/risk reasoning rather than a false single “best” journal.

Creating or submitting the final submission version is a mandatory pause.

## 4. Citation and submission finalization

**Route:** `communication-writing`, `communication-literature`, and
`communication-reviewer`; use a reference manager only with explicit external-write
authority.

For every material citation, separately verify:

1. **existence and identity:** title, authors, venue, year, DOI/stable ID;
2. **claim support:** the source actually supports the nearby wording, population, direction, magnitude, and causal strength.

Then check in-text/bibliography bijection, duplicates, retractions/corrections where material, style, locators, numbers, tables, figures, supplements, anonymization, data/code statements, and target-journal requirements.

Final delivery requires fresh document/code/data validation and a read of the output. A clean bibliography does not prove claim support; a passing render does not prove numerical consistency.
