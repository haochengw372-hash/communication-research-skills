# Scale adaptation and invariance checklist

## Evidence-gathering

- [ ] Original scale paper located with full items and scoring.
- [ ] Validation samples recorded (size, population, language, country).
- [ ] Reliability reported for both alpha and omega when available.
- [ ] Validity evidence classified: content, structural (CFA/EFA), convergent/discriminant, criterion.
- [ ] Existing translations checked before new translation is commissioned.

## Adaptation steps (in order)

1. Forward translation by qualified translator (target language).
2. Independent back-translation by a different translator.
3. Expert committee review: discrepancy log, construct/item/semantic equivalence.
4. Cognitive pretest with target-population respondents (each item probed).
5. Pilot test: item distributions, response-process problems, provisional reliability.
6. Full-data CFA of the intended factor structure.
7. Measurement invariance testing (multi-group):
   - configural (same pattern);
   - metric (equal loadings) — minimum for comparing relations;
   - scalar (equal intercepts) — required before comparing means;
   - record fit and change-in-fit criteria and the chosen threshold.
8. Reliability on the new sample and item-level report.

## Item modification rules

Any of these triggers full or partial revalidation rather than "adapted from":

- wording change;
- response anchors changed;
- item order/material context changed;
- short form or item deletion;
- reverse-coded items removed;
- medium-specific referents changed (e.g., "news" to "platform feed") without a measurement study.

## Group-comparison rules

- Do not compare latent means without scalar invariance.
- Do not compare regression coefficients across groups without at least metric invariance and a stated assumption.
- When invariance fails, report which items/intercepts failed and treat the comparison as exploratory, not confirmatory.

## Output

End with the scale sheet from the main SKILL.md plus an adaptation log with the invariance table and the final decision.
