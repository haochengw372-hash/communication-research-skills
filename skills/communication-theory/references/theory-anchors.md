# Communication theory anchors

Read this table when a candidate theory is among the common anchors. Each row states the mechanism, boundary conditions, typical suitable evidence, and common misuse. This is a routing aid, not a substitute for the original theoretical literature.

| Theory / perspective | Mechanism | Boundary conditions | Suitable evidence | Common misuse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CASA / Media Equation | Humans mindlessly apply social rules to computers when social cues are present | Requires perceivable human-like cues; applies to automatic, not deliberate, processing | Experiments manipulating social cues and measuring social responses | Claiming any AI interaction is "social" without cues or automaticity |
| Warranting theory | People trust information to the extent it is hard for the target to manipulate | Applies to identity claims and self-presentation contexts | Experiments varying warranting value of online cues | Using "warranting" for any credibility judgment unrelated to identity claims |
| Attribution theory / accountability inference | Observers infer authorship, responsibility, and character from cues and outcome information | Requires an attribution target and perceived agency | Experiments varying disclosure/agency cues and measuring responsibility or trust | Treating attribution as a synonym for general liking |
| Agenda-setting | Media attention transfers issue salience to audiences | Applies to issue salience over time; first/second level differ | Content × survey or time-series designs | Inferring salience transfer from content frequency alone |
| Framing | Presentational emphasis changes interpretation | Requires equivalent core information with different emphasis | Experiments or content-design comparison | Labeling any positive/negative tone a "frame" |
| Cultivation | Heavy media exposure shapes perceived social reality | Traditionally mainstream TV; needs boundary revision for platforms | Exposure × perception surveys with controls | Claiming cultivation from content patterns with no audience data |
| Uses & gratifications | Media use is motivated by needs and selective | Audience-active contexts | Survey/latent-class designs linking motives to use | Using it post hoc to explain any effect without measuring motives |
| Social presence | Medium conveys psychological co-presence of others | Especially relevant to mediated interaction and AI agents | Experiments measuring perceived presence and outcomes | Equating presence with any positive evaluation |
| Uncertainty reduction | Strangers reduce uncertainty through information seeking | Initial interactions; has assumptions about typical communication | Interaction experiments and message designs | Applying it to repeated, relationship-saturated interactions |
| Privacy calculus | Disclosure follows weighing benefits and risks | Requires defined privacy beliefs and benefit perceptions | Surveys and experiments measuring disclosure tradeoffs | Using it as a label for any consent behavior |
| MAIN model | Technological affordances (modality, agency, interactivity, navigability) cue heuristic judgments | Applies to credibility heuristics triggered by interface cues | Experiments varying one affordance at a time | Confounding affordances (agency cue bundled with different modality) |
| Machine-heuristic / algorithmic perception (HMC stream) | Perceived machine or algorithmic origins activate distinct expectations about competence, agency, and warmth | AI-visible interactions where users infer the actor | Experiments isolating machine-origin cues from content differences | Assuming users always notice or always distrust the machine origin |
| Gatekeeping | Actors and routines select, shape, and sequence information before it reaches publics | Requires an identifiable selection stage and decision authority | Newsroom/process traces, selection models, interviews plus output comparison | Calling any content difference gatekeeping without observing selection |
| Diffusion of innovations | Adoption depends on perceived attributes, communication channels, social systems, and time | Concerns adoption trajectories; stages and adopter categories are not fixed personality types | Longitudinal adoption data, network diffusion, comparative case evidence | Treating awareness, trial, and sustained adoption as the same outcome |
| Two-step flow / opinion leadership | Information and influence move through socially positioned intermediaries | Requires evidence that intermediaries are exposed and subsequently influence others | Panel-network data, field experiments, temporal sharing and exposure records | Inferring opinion leadership from follower count or centrality alone |
| Spiral of silence | Perceived opinion climate and fear of isolation inhibit public expression | Requires a salient opinion climate and a socially risky expression context | Surveys/experiments measuring perceived climate, fear, and willingness to speak | Treating low posting frequency as silence without measuring perceived climate |
| Selective exposure | People preferentially encounter or choose attitude-consistent information | Choice must be possible; exposure supplied by algorithms is not necessarily selection | Choice experiments, browsing logs joined to prior attitudes, panels | Inferring preference from observed exposure without separating supply and choice |
| Knowledge gap | Information growth can widen knowledge differences when socioeconomic resources shape acquisition | Requires comparable information opportunity and longitudinal or repeated measurement | Repeated surveys, interventions, exposure-by-resource models | Treating any group mean difference as a growing gap |
| Elaboration Likelihood Model / Heuristic-Systematic Model | Motivation and ability shift processing between effortful evaluation and heuristic cues | Routes are not fixed person types; issue involvement and ability must be measured or manipulated | Message experiments with argument-quality and cue manipulations | Labeling any small effect a heuristic without testing processing conditions |
| Social cognitive / observational learning | People learn behaviors and outcome expectations by observing models and consequences | Requires attention, retention, model relevance, and an actionable behavior | Experiments or longitudinal exposure-behavior studies | Equating content exposure with imitation |
| Media-system dependency | Media effects grow when people depend on media resources to understand or act under uncertainty | Dependency is relational and context-specific, not frequency alone | Crisis/event panels, dependency scales, comparative media-system designs | Using heavy media use as a proxy for dependency |
| Communication affordances | Perceived action possibilities arise from relations among technology, users, and context | Affordances are neither platform features alone nor guaranteed behaviors | Comparative interface studies, walkthroughs, experiments, trace-plus-interview designs | Naming any feature an affordance without the actor-action relation |
| Public sphere / counterpublics | Communication arenas organize visibility, legitimacy, participation, and contestation | Normative criteria and boundaries of the public must be explicit | Network/content/discourse evidence plus access and participation analysis | Treating any online group as a public sphere or any minority cluster as a counterpublic |
| Mediatization / platformization | Media and platform logics reorganize institutions, practices, and dependencies over time | Requires institutional or field-level change, not isolated technology use | Longitudinal organizational studies, policy/infrastructure analysis, comparative cases | Using the term as a synonym for digitization |
| Algorithmic imaginaries | Actors' beliefs about algorithms guide adaptation even when system knowledge is incomplete | Claims concern consequential beliefs, not the true algorithm | Interviews, diaries, experiments, behavioral traces joined to stated beliefs | Treating inaccurate beliefs as analytically irrelevant or as system facts |
| Journalistic / epistemic authority | Journalism's authority rests on recognized practices for producing and validating public knowledge | Requires a journalism or knowledge-production object and an identifiable authority claim | News production studies, source/verification analysis, audience experiments | Reducing authority to credibility ratings without institutional or epistemic mechanisms |
| Network agenda-setting | Salience transfers among networks of issues and attributes rather than isolated objects | Requires comparable issue/attribute networks and a defensible temporal relation | Dynamic semantic networks joined to audience or cross-media salience data | Inferring transfer from contemporaneous network similarity |

## Using the table

1. Match the phenomenon to one row by mechanism, not by keyword.
2. Copy the boundary conditions into the theory memo.
3. If two rows both fit, choose the one whose falsifiable prediction the design can distinguish.
4. If none fit, do not force an anchor: search the original literature for the mechanism rather than reusing this starter set.
